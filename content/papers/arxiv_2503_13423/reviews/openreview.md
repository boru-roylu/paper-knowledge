- title: Intuitive extension of BPE algorithm to achieve faster and better inference quality
- rating: 7
- confidence: 3
- review: ## Key Contributions The core contribution of "SuperBPE: Space Travel for Language Models" is the introduction of SuperBPE, a novel tokenization algorithm that learns "superword" tokens—multi-word expressions treated as single units—thereby challenging the conventional assumption that tokens must be confined within word boundaries. This method aims to create more semantically cohesive and efficient token sequences for language models. ## Strengths Improved Encoding Efficiency: SuperBPE significantly reduces the number of tokens required to encode text, achieving up to 33% fewer tokens on average compared to traditional BPE, especially beneficial with limited vocabulary sizes. Enhanced Downstream Performance: Language models pretrained with SuperBPE demonstrate substantial improvements across various downstream tasks (e.g., +4.0% absolute improvement over BPE baselines across 30 tasks, and +8.2% on MMLU), while also reducing inference compute by 27%. Semantic Cohesion: By forming superwords, the algorithm groups semantically related words, potentially leading to a more uniform per-token difficulty in predictions and better representation of multi-word expressions. Ease of Integration: SuperBPE is designed as a straightforward modification to tokenization, requiring no changes to existing model architectures, training frameworks, or decoding strategies, which facilitates its adoption. ## Limitations and Weaknesses Computational Overhead for Training: The two-stage pretokenization curriculum, particularly the phase allowing merges across whitespace, might introduce additional computational complexity or time during the tokenizer's training phase compared to standard BPE. Language Specificity: While the paper mentions languages without whitespace, the primary evaluation seems to be focused on English. The performance benefits might vary across different languages, especially those with complex morphology or agglutinative structures, which could present unique challenges for learning "superwords." Vocabulary Size Impact: Although the paper states that SuperBPE's advantage grows with larger vocabulary sizes, there might be a threshold beyond which the gains diminish or the vocabulary becomes too specific, potentially leading to issues with generalization to unseen text. Unknown Effect on the Large LLMs. The experimentation was done with 8B parameters LLMs. Large LLMs (with > 100B parameters) generalize well. It is not certain if similar performance gains would be observed if ablation study was done using larger LLMs. ## Overall Assessment This paper presents a valuable and practical advancement in language model tokenization. By creatively extending BPE to incorporate "superwords," the research successfully addresses a long-standing limitation of subword tokenization, leading to measurable improvements in encoding efficiency and downstream model performance. Its strength lies in offering a direct and impactful solution without requiring complex architectural changes. While the paper doesn't delve into potential training overhead or language sensitivity, the demonstrated gains in efficiency and performance, coupled with its ease of integration, position SuperBPE as a significant contribution to the field of natural language processing. It provides a compelling argument for moving beyond strict subword boundaries in tokenization.

- review: This paper presents a new tokenization algorithm that is a variation on the widely-used BPE. In this algorithm tokenization is done in 2 stages. Stage 1: normal BPE; and stage 2: removal of the constraints on white-space a token delimiter. The paper shows that SuperBPE compresses texts better than BPE and that, given the amount of FLOPs for training, models with SuperBPE outperform models trained with a vanilla BPE. The work in this paper is solid and it addresses well the arbitrariness of white spaces, especially from a cross-lingual POV. I didn't spot any major flaws in this work, although I think that an equi-steps comparison will be more informative (see below). ## some discussions I miss in the paper To be fully convinced in the superiority of SuperBPE I would like to see a comparison when both models go through the same number of steps, probably in addition to equi-FLOPs comparison shown in the paper. I understand the reasoning in the setting of the paper. Its conclusion is that given a training budget in FLOPs, one is better advised to use SuperBPE. But I want to know whether SuperBPE is also superior from a more theoretical point of view. Is it better in modelling language? Does it represent language in a way that is "truer" in some sense? Will it be better even in infinite compute? Is it better independently from allowing more steps? How come 80k is the best for encoding efficiency? From figure 1 it seems like encoding is more efficient, the earlier you switch to stage 2, is it not? Earlier transition also makes sense from the standpoint of language, because it seems like the transition itself is only an approximation that is done due to the memory complexity of training completely without pretokenization. - title: Good idea and extensive evaluations of the proposed idea but would have loved to see how this extends to languages like Chinese which does not have a concept of whitespace
- rating: 6
- confidence: 4
- review: ## Summary The paper presents a simple idea: during the BPE process, switch from operating on pre-tokenized words and then lift the restriction to learn super words. This makes the model learn better semantic constructs, like concepts which are explained by multiple words, makes LLM inference faster, and achieve better performance on series of downstream tasks. The paper presents multiple experimental results and analysis to justify the claims made in this paper. ## Strengths - The paper clearly explains the simple yet useful idea of going from subwords to superwords at a transition point in the BPE generation phase to encode more common superwords as a single token. - The evaluations and analysis is extensive and proper care has been done to maintain the control variables across the various settings like number of training FLOPs, effective context length etc. - Makes the LLM inference faster and cheaper. ## Weaknesses - The paper talks about languages that do not use whitespace (like Chinese) and how the cross lingual interactions with English where the translation can span into multiple words across whitespace boundary. In my opinion, an evaluation like translation of words of one language (say Chinese) to English would have been a good evaluation to add to strengthen the claims made in this paper and enforce its relevance in the age of multi lingual LLMs - This is the main reason behind marking the paper as marginal since it does not talk about the multi-lingual aspect of the problem after the abstract and introduction sections. Novelty seems to be there but its impact and usage in the current LLM space seems limited and needs more testing at that scale to understand whether the benefits would hold.
